# Overhead and maximum rates on current builds (v0.7.0, 2026-11-08)

`scripts/bench-v070-overhead.sh` re-measures p11scope's real cost and
maximum exact rates on the v0.7.0 integration tip. This supersedes the
v0.1.0 numbers (`docs/notes/phase5-overhead.md`, kept for history).

## Plain-language summary

On the quiet Fedora VM (5 vCPU), watching SoftHSM2 cost, per call:
metrics +4.7 µs, inventory --pid +6.4 µs, trace +6.8 µs, profile
+7.5 µs — against ~0.9 µs unobserved. In throughput terms: an app
doing 100k fast calls/s costs the observer roughly 4% of one core
(metrics), 12% (inventory counts), or three-quarters of a core
(profile/trace event capture, scaled from the measured ~130k/s
point). 1M exact-counted calls/s is reached (metrics/profile
aggregates at 1.2M/s across 3 providers × 12 threads on the busy dev
box, 0.3 observer cores; quiet Fedora holds 970k/s exact on 5
vCPUs). p11scope beats OpenSC's pkcs11-spy shim on
every count-mode workload (up to 28× vs spy-to-file on fast threaded
calls — the shim stops scaling past 1–2 threads); trace ties or
trails the shim by ≤17% only on slow sub-ms calls. Two honest
caveats: per-call event capture loses events in the hundreds of k/s
(9.5% at 351k/s busy, exact to 437k/s quiet), and `inventory
--system` bound zero usage rows anywhere — its counts are currently
unmeasurable, not merely slow (§5).

Median + min..max ns/call over 5 interleaved rounds, 1M
`C_GenerateRandom` calls after 1k warm-up (Fedora), plus a heavier
HMAC `C_Sign` (200k ops; each op is 2 calls, `C_SignInit`+`C_Sign`,
so per-call cost is half the per-op delta). Observer CPU is user+sys
of the p11scope process per second of workload, as a fraction of one
core.

## 4. p11perftest / pkcs11-spy cross-checks

Same p11perftest matrix (coverages rand, ecdsa, rsa, aes; threads
1..all vCPUs) under: plain SoftHSM2; p11scope `inventory --pid`;
p11scope `profile --mode metrics`; p11scope `trace`; pkcs11-spy to
file; pkcs11-spy to /dev/null. Timed `pkcs11-tool --sign` /
`p11tool --test-sign` loops included.

Observed configs STOP-start p11perftest with the provider preloaded
(`LD_PRELOAD` + maps gate) so the observer covers the whole run;
without the preload the attach gap blends unobserved calls into the
headline rate. Counts are p11perftest's own timed iterations vs each
tool's observed total. `C_Initialize` (plus ≤7 more startup calls)
sneaks through before the STOP lands on every observed run, so all
STOP-started tools agree with each other to the call but sit ≤8
below spy's in-process ground truth; timed-call capture is complete
in all of them (see below).

### Dev (busy host), rand coverage (100k iters × 2 tests)

ops/s per configuration (higher is faster):

| Threads | plain | inv-pid | metrics | trace | spy-null | spy-file |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 930k | 119k | 140k | 113k | 56k | 32k |
| 12 | 770k | 133k | 207k | 351k | 59k | 25k |

p11scope is 2–4× faster than pkcs11-spy to /dev/null and 3–6× faster
than spy-to-file here: uprobe traps (~6–9 µs) beat in-process
interposition + formatting + (for file) IO. The shim does not scale
with threads (59k at t12 vs 56k at t1 — its own lock) and degrades
with run length (t1 spy-null halved when iters doubled 50k→100k,
while p11scope held steady), pointing at an O(n) per-call structure
in the shim rather than pure IO cost.

Count agreement (t1 totals, spy ground truth 220019): inv-pid
220011, metrics 220011, trace 220011 — all three miss only the ≤8
pre-STOP startup calls; all 220000 timed calls captured. metrics
reports `COMPLETE` (its window starts at attach); trace reports
`PARTIAL` because its loader-discovery flags the pre-STOP window —
same data, different sensitivity. At t12 (spy 2640129): metrics
2640121 (−8, sneak only), inv-pid 2640086 (−43, +35 ring/admission,
`lossy` flag set), trace 2390531 (−9.5% real event loss at 351k/s).

## 5. `inventory --system` binds no usage rows (both hosts)

Every `--system` cell on both hosts reported zero bound rows
(`native_witnesses rows=0`, edges `not_attached`), across workload
shapes: single 200 ms bursts, 10 s continuous runs at 400–900k/s,
and 50-op CLI-tool loops. The probes do attach (6802 endpoints on
Fedora) and the semantic lane sees the calls (14k joined on a 20k
burst), but no usage-count rows bind, so there is no per-call column
for `--system` in §1. Per-call cost of the attached-but-unbound
probes is still visible in the workload wall time (Fedora percall
r1: 8483 ns/call vs 1120 unobserved, i.e. +7.4 µs of trap cost with
zero rows bound). Product follow-up needed; the cells are kept as
INVALID evidence, not dropped.

## 6. Method notes and honest gaps

- `inventory --system` bound zero rows for every workload shape
  (single bursts, 10 s continuous to 1.1M/s, CLI loops) — see §5.
- Slowdown numbers come from each workload's own timers over fully
  observed runs (STOP-start + preload + maps gate); a `PARTIAL`
  verdict affects the count-agreement columns, not the rates.
- STOP-starting costs ≤8 pre-STOP startup calls per run
  (`C_Initialize` etc.); all timed calls are captured. This is why
  STOP-started tools sit ≤8 below spy's in-process ground truth.
- Semantic (per-key) attribution is lossy under burst by design
  (~15% joined at ~1M/s: 383k of 2.64M on rand t12); count lanes are
  reported separately and stay exact where stated.
- Both hosts run other lanes concurrently; the script waits out
  foreign captures before each attach (600 s cap, `FOREIGN-RISK`
  flag if lapsed — never triggered in the final runs) and respawns
  the observer once on attach failure. Dev numbers carry a "busy
  host" label (load ~5–16 during the runs).
- Fedora ramp is complete except mt5 `inv-system` (observer
  singleton aborted the run at the last ramp cell; foregone INVALID).
  One ceiling respawn recovered; no `FOREIGN-RISK` lapses.
